# Indexed universal life (IUL): honest education

IUL is permanent life insurance with flexible premiums and a cash value whose interest credit is linked to the performance of a market index, such as the S&P 500, without the money being invested in the market directly. It combines a death benefit with potential cash value growth, and it requires disciplined, consistent funding.

## How the crediting works

- Each crediting period the policy looks at the index change and credits interest based on that change, limited by a cap rate (the maximum credited), a participation rate (the share of the index gain used) and sometimes a spread.
- A floor, often 0 percent, means index losses are not credited as negative interest. This does not mean the cash value cannot go down: policy charges (cost of insurance, administrative and rider charges) are deducted every month regardless of the index, so in years with low or zero credits the cash value can fall.
- Caps and participation rates are usually not guaranteed and can be changed by the carrier within contract limits.
- Dividends from the index are typically not included in the index change used for crediting.

## Illustrations and regulation

Sales illustrations project future values using assumed rates. Actuarial Guidelines 49-A and 49-B limit the rates and loan assumptions that illustrations can show, because aggressive illustrations misled consumers in the past. An illustration is a hypothetical projection, never a promise. The licensed agent must show both the guaranteed and the non-guaranteed columns.

## Taxes, loans and the MEC line

- The death benefit is generally income tax free to beneficiaries.
- Cash value can be accessed through withdrawals and policy loans, often with favorable tax treatment if the policy is not a modified endowment contract (MEC) and stays in force until death.
- Overfunding beyond federal limits turns the policy into a MEC, which changes how loans and withdrawals are taxed.
- Loans accrue interest. If loans plus charges exceed the cash value, the policy can lapse and the gain can become taxable. Lapse risk is the most important risk to explain.
- Tax rules are complex: the person should confirm with a tax professional.

## Who it may fit

People with a permanent coverage need, stable income, a long time horizon, who can fund the policy consistently and understand the moving parts. It is often discussed for supplemental retirement income (sometimes called a LIRP), which is only appropriate when the policy is properly designed and funded, and never as a replacement for emergency savings.

## Lines that never move

- Never promise that the cash value can only go up, never describe it as market gains without risk, and never promise tax free retirement income.
- Never compare it to a 401(k) or a brokerage account as if it were the same kind of product.
- Never steer toward IUL because it pays higher commission. The licensed agent decides after seeing the full picture.
