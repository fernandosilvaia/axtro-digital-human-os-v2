# Premium financing and the Kai-Zen strategy

Premium financing means a third party lender pays part of the premiums of a life insurance policy, usually an indexed universal life policy, with the policy itself as collateral. Kai-Zen is a registered name of a premium financing strategy offered through specific distribution partners for high-income professionals. The goal it is designed for is more death benefit and more potential cash value for future supplemental income than the person could fund alone.

## How the typical design is described

The structure below is how program materials commonly describe it. Exact terms change, and the official program documents and the carrier illustration always prevail.

- The participant pays premiums for an initial period, often described as five years.
- A lender finances additional premiums for a period, which is the leverage.
- The loan accrues interest. Illustrations commonly project the loan being repaid from the policy's cash value years later, often shown around year 15.
- After the loan is repaid, the remaining cash value can be accessed through policy loans or withdrawals, with the same tax conditions and lapse risks as any IUL.

## Who it is designed for

Typically working professionals with high, stable income who qualify financially and medically, often within an age range set by the program. They must go through full underwriting and a financial review. It is not designed for people who need the money soon, cannot commit to the contribution period or do not understand leverage.

## The risks that must always be explained

- Interest rate risk: if lending rates rise, the cost of the loan rises.
- Performance risk: if index credits are lower than the illustration assumed, the policy may not be able to repay the loan when projected.
- Collateral and contribution risk: depending on the program terms, the participant may be asked for more money or collateral if the policy underperforms. The licensed specialist must explain exactly what the participant is and is not responsible for.
- Lapse risk: an underfunded IUL can lapse, which can create taxable gain.
- Complexity: caps, participation rates, loan terms and program rules can change.

## How Priscila handles it

- Explain the idea in one or two sentences, without numbers: "It uses bank financing to help fund a larger policy, which can help or hurt depending on rates and performance, so it needs a specialist review."
- Ask two qualifying questions: whether their income is stable and whether they could commit to several years of contributions.
- Route to the licensed specialist. Never estimate returns, never say "free money", "the bank pays for your policy" or "no risk", and never compare it to an investment account.
