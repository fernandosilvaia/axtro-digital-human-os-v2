# Premium financing and the Kai-Zen strategy

Premium financing means a lender pays part of the premiums of a life insurance policy, usually an indexed universal life policy. The loan is typically secured by the policy and sometimes by extra collateral. Kai-Zen is a branded strategy name used by specific distribution partners for high-income professionals. The program documents and the carrier illustration, not Priscila, describe what it does and what it costs.

## What is and is not known

- Descriptions of Kai-Zen come from the program's own distributors, and they differ on details such as income thresholds, minimum contributions, the contribution period and who signs the loan. No independent review of outcomes was found.
- Because those descriptions disagree, Priscila does not quote years, amounts, repayment dates or who is personally responsible for the loan. She sends the person to the licensed specialist and the official documents.
- Generally, premium finance loans are repaid from the policy's cash value or from other funds years later, and illustrations often show a long horizon. Whether that works depends on interest rates and on how the policy performs.

## Who it is designed for

Typically working professionals with high, stable income who qualify financially and medically, often within an age range set by the program. They must go through full underwriting and a financial review. It is not designed for people who need the money soon, cannot commit to the contribution period or do not understand borrowing.

## The risks that must always be explained

- Interest rate risk: loan rates are often floating and reset over time, and unpaid interest can be added to the loan.
- Performance risk: if index credits are lower than the illustration assumed, the policy may not be able to repay the loan when projected.
- Collateral risk: a lender may ask for more collateral or money if the policy underperforms, and in some structures can call the loan. The licensed specialist must explain exactly what the participant is and is not responsible for.
- Lapse risk: an underfunded IUL can lapse, which can create taxable gain.
- Tax and legal rules: some structures fall under federal split-dollar rules, interest deductions are limited, and a policy must have a real insurable interest. A tax professional and the specialist decide what applies.
- Complexity: caps, participation rates, loan terms and program rules can change.

## How Priscila handles it

- Explain the idea in one or two sentences, without numbers: "It uses borrowed money to help fund a larger policy, which can help or hurt depending on rates and performance, so it needs a specialist review."
- Ask two qualifying questions: whether their income is stable and whether they could commit to several years of contributions.
- Route to the licensed specialist. Never estimate returns, never say "free money", "the bank pays for your policy" or "no risk", and never compare it to an investment account.
