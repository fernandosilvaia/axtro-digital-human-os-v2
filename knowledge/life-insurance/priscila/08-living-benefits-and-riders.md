# Living benefits and riders

Riders are options added to a life insurance policy. Some are included at no extra premium by certain carriers, others cost extra. Availability, triggers and amounts vary by carrier and state, and the licensed agent confirms them.

## Living benefits (accelerated death benefit riders)

They let the insured receive part of the death benefit while alive after a qualifying event. Whatever is paid early reduces the death benefit the family receives later.

- Terminal illness: a diagnosis with a limited life expectancy defined in the rider, often 12 or 24 months.
- Chronic illness: usually inability to perform a set number of activities of daily living (such as bathing, dressing, eating) or severe cognitive impairment, certified by a physician.
- Critical illness: specific diagnoses listed in the rider, such as certain heart attacks, strokes, cancers or organ failures.

Why it matters: many families fear a long illness more than an early death. Living benefits can help with income, medical costs or home care during that time. It is not health insurance and not long-term care insurance, unless the rider is specifically a long-term care rider.

## Other common riders

- Waiver of premium: premiums are waived if the insured becomes disabled as defined in the rider.
- Accidental death benefit: pays an additional amount if death is caused by a covered accident.
- Child term rider: small term coverage on children, often convertible later.
- Return of premium: on some term policies, refunds premiums if the insured outlives the term with the policy in force.
- Guaranteed insurability: lets the insured buy more coverage at set times without new medical questions.
- Long-term care rider or hybrid life and long-term care: pays for qualifying long-term care costs from the death benefit or a separate pool, under the rules of the contract.

## How to bring it up naturally

- "Many people tell me they worry more about a long illness than about dying early. Would it matter to you if part of the coverage could help while you are still here?"
- Keep it as an option the agent can show, never as a promise that a specific condition is covered.
