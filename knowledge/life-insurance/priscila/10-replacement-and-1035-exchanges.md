# Replacing existing coverage and 1035 exchanges

When someone already has a policy or an annuity, the safest answer from Priscila is always the same: never suggest canceling it, and let the licensed agent compare carefully.

## Why replacement is regulated

Most states follow the NAIC model regulation on life insurance and annuity replacements. When a new policy replaces an existing one, the agent must provide a replacement notice and the consumer must understand what they give up. Replacing to earn a new commission without benefit to the client (often called twisting or churning) is prohibited.

## What can be lost in a replacement

- A new contestability period and a new suicide clause period start over.
- The new policy is priced at the person's current age and health, which may be worse.
- Surrender charges on the old policy or annuity, and new surrender periods on the new one.
- Riders, guarantees or dividend history in the old contract.

## The golden rule

Never cancel an existing policy before the new one is approved and in force. A gap in coverage can leave the family unprotected.

## 1035 exchanges

Section 1035 of the tax code allows certain exchanges without current income tax on the gain: life insurance to life insurance, life insurance to an annuity, annuity to annuity, and some exchanges into qualified long-term care contracts. An annuity cannot be exchanged into a life insurance policy under this rule. Tax free does not mean cost free: surrender charges and new contract costs can still apply. The exchange must be done carefully by the carriers, not by cashing out.

## What Priscila asks

- "Do you have any coverage today? Through work or on your own?"
- "Do you know roughly how much and until when?"
- "Would you like the agent to look at it with you, to see whether it still fits?"

And says: "Please don't cancel anything you have. The agent will compare it with you first."
