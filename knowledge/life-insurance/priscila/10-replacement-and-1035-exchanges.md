# Replacing existing coverage and 1035 exchanges

When someone already has a policy or an annuity, the safest answer from Priscila is always the same: never suggest canceling it, and let the licensed agent compare carefully.

## Why replacement is regulated

Most states follow the NAIC model regulation on life insurance and annuity replacements. When a new policy replaces an existing one, the agent must provide a replacement notice and the consumer must understand what they give up. Replacing to earn a new commission without benefit to the client (often called twisting or churning) is prohibited.

## What can be lost in a replacement

- A new contestability period starts, and a new suicide clause period often starts too, unless the replacement is with the same company.
- The new policy is priced at the person's current age and health, which may be worse.
- Surrender charges on the old policy or annuity, and new surrender periods on the new one.
- Riders, guarantees or dividend history in the old contract.

## The golden rule

Priscila never suggests canceling an existing policy. The agent never cancels an old policy before the new one is approved and in force, because a gap in coverage can leave the family unprotected.

## 1035 exchanges

Section 1035 of the tax code allows certain exchanges without current income tax on the gain: life insurance to life insurance, life insurance to an annuity, annuity to annuity, and some exchanges into qualified long-term care contracts. An annuity cannot be exchanged into a life insurance policy under this rule. The exchange must be done directly between the carriers on the same insured, not by cashing out, and the cost basis carries over. Tax free does not mean cost free: surrender charges, new acquisition costs and a new contestability period can still apply. A policy received in exchange for a modified endowment contract (MEC) is also a MEC.

## What Priscila asks

- "Do you have any coverage today? Through work or on your own?"
- "Do you know roughly how much and until when?"
- "Would you like the agent to look at it with you, to see whether it still fits?"

And says: "Before changing or canceling anything you have, let the agent compare it with you first."
