# Final expense insurance: what it is and how to talk about it

Final expense is a small whole life policy, usually a few thousand to a few tens of thousands of dollars of coverage, bought to pay for a funeral, burial or cremation, medical bills and small debts so the family does not carry them. It is most common between ages 50 and 85. Premiums stay level for life and the coverage does not expire as long as premiums are paid.

## The three ways it is issued

- Simplified issue, level benefit: a short health questionnaire, no medical exam. If the answers qualify, the full death benefit is available from day one.
- Simplified issue, graded or modified benefit: for some health histories. During the first two years, a death from natural causes usually pays a partial benefit or a return of premiums paid plus interest. Accidental death usually pays the full amount from the start. After the waiting period, the full benefit applies.
- Guaranteed issue (guaranteed acceptance): no health questions within the age range. Because anyone can get it, it usually has a two year limited benefit period: a natural death in that period typically returns premiums plus interest, not the full face amount.

Exact rules, ages, amounts and waiting periods vary by carrier and state. The licensed agent confirms which option the person qualifies for.

## Why people buy it

- Funeral costs in the United States commonly run several thousand dollars and can reach ten thousand or more depending on the state and the choices made. Families often pay that within days.
- It is quick: many applications are decided in days with a phone interview.
- Premiums do not increase with age once the policy is issued.
- Some carriers include a free funeral planning service or let the family receive the money directly; the beneficiary decides how to use it.

## Questions that help

- "If you passed away, who would be in charge of the arrangements?"
- "Have you thought about burial or cremation?"
- "Do you have anything set aside for that today, or any policy from work or the bank?"
- "What monthly amount would feel comfortable?"
- Health stays basic: tobacco use, and whether they have been treated for major conditions recently. The detailed history belongs to the application with the licensed agent.

## Talking with seniors

Slow down, use short sentences and repeat the essentials. Offer to include a son, daughter or trusted person. Never rush, never say the price goes up tomorrow unless it is true and specific (for example, a birthday that changes the age band, which the agent confirms). Never imply a connection with Medicare, Social Security or any government program.

## Common objections, answered honestly

- "I have money saved." Respect it. Ask whether that money is meant for something else and whether the family knows where it is and can access it quickly.
- "My kids will take care of it." Ask whether they have talked about it and how they would feel paying on short notice.
- "I'm too old." Many carriers accept applicants into their 80s; the agent confirms.
- "I have a health problem." Simplified issue has options for many conditions, and guaranteed issue exists when nothing else fits.
- "Is this a scam?" Invite them to verify the agent's license on their state Department of Insurance website and never ask for bank details on the call.
