# Final expense insurance: what it is and how to talk about it

Final expense is a small whole life policy, usually a few thousand to a few tens of thousands of dollars of coverage, bought to pay for a funeral, burial or cremation, medical bills and small debts so the family does not carry them. Issue ages vary by carrier and state: published ranges run from about 45 to 85, and are narrower in some states. Premiums stay level for life and the coverage does not expire as long as premiums are paid.

## The three ways it is issued

- Simplified issue, level benefit: a short health questionnaire, no medical exam. If the answers qualify, the full death benefit is available from day one.
- Simplified issue, graded or modified benefit: for some health histories. During the first two years, a death from natural causes usually pays a reduced benefit, often the premiums paid plus a set percentage (110 percent of premiums is common) or sometimes plus interest, depending on the carrier. Accidental death usually pays the full amount from the start. After the waiting period, the full benefit applies.
- Guaranteed issue (guaranteed acceptance): no health questions within the age range. Because acceptance is guaranteed, it usually costs more for the same amount of coverage and has a two year limited benefit period, when a natural death typically returns the premiums paid plus a set percentage or interest, not the full face amount.

In a 2025 industry survey of 30 carriers (LIMRA and Life Insurers Council), 76 percent of reported final expense sales were simplified issue, with an average face amount of about 15 thousand dollars, and 24 percent were guaranteed issue, with an average of about 11 thousand dollars. Exact rules, ages, amounts and waiting periods vary by carrier and state. The licensed agent confirms which option the person qualifies for.

## Why people buy it

- The latest funeral industry price study (NFDA, 2023) found typical median costs of about 8,300 dollars for a funeral with viewing and burial and about 6,300 dollars for viewing and cremation. Cemetery, monument and cash advance items are not included, so the total is often higher.
- Social Security pays a one-time death payment of 255 dollars, only to a surviving spouse or some minor children. That is far below funeral costs, and no government program pays for this insurance.
- It is quick: many applications are decided in days with a phone interview.
- Premiums do not increase with age once the policy is issued.
- The beneficiary receives the money directly and decides how to use it.

## Questions that help

- "If you passed away, who would be in charge of the arrangements?"
- "Have you thought about burial or cremation?"
- "Do you have anything set aside for that today, or any policy from work or the bank?"
- "What monthly amount would feel comfortable?"
- Health stays basic: tobacco use, and whether they have been treated for major conditions recently. The detailed history belongs to the application with the licensed agent.

## Talking with seniors

Slow down, use short sentences and repeat the essentials. Offer to include a son, daughter or trusted person. Never rush and never create urgency. If age or a birthday affects the quote, say that the agent confirms how. Never imply a connection with Medicare, Social Security or any government program.

## Common objections, answered honestly

- "I have money saved." Respect it. Ask whether that money is set aside for this purpose or meant for something else.
- "My kids will take care of it." Ask whether they have talked about it, and whether the family knows what the person wants.
- "I'm too old." Many carriers accept applicants into their 80s; the agent confirms.
- "I have a health problem." Simplified issue has options for many conditions. Guaranteed issue exists for people who cannot qualify for other options, with a higher cost for the coverage and the two year limited benefit.
- "Is this a scam?" Invite them to verify the agent's license on their state Department of Insurance website, and never ask for bank details on the call.
