# Term life and mortgage protection

Term life covers a person for a fixed number of years, commonly 10, 15, 20, 25 or 30. If they die during the term, the beneficiaries receive the death benefit. If the term ends, coverage ends unless it is renewed (the price resets to the person's current age and is usually higher) or converted. It is the lowest cost per dollar of coverage, which makes it the usual answer for temporary, large needs: income replacement, a mortgage, children until they are independent, a business loan.

## Key features to explain

- Level premium: the premium stays the same for the whole term on a level term policy.
- Conversion: many term policies let the owner convert to a permanent policy without new medical questions, within a conversion window set by the carrier, usually shorter than the term. Valuable if health changes later.
- Return of premium term: costs more, and refunds the premiums paid if the person outlives the term and kept the policy in force until the end. The refund conditions vary by carrier.
- Living benefits riders: many term policies can pay part of the death benefit early after a qualifying terminal, chronic or critical illness. See the living benefits document.
- Underwriting: from fully underwritten (exam, labs) to accelerated or no-exam options for healthier applicants within carrier limits. Rate classes go from preferred to standard and table ratings, with separate tobacco rates.

## How much coverage: a simple, transparent estimate

A common rule of thumb is DIME: Debts, plus Income (years the family would need it times annual income), plus Mortgage balance, plus Education costs, minus existing coverage and savings set aside. Another method, human life value, estimates the present value of future income. Both are educational estimates, never a recommendation, and neither accounts for everything. The licensed agent refines it with the person's real situation.

Example to explain the logic, not to quote: someone earning 50 thousand a year who wants to give the family 10 years of income, with a 200 thousand mortgage and 20 thousand of other debts, comes to about 720 thousand dollars in this example, before adding education costs and before subtracting what they already have.

## Mortgage protection

Mortgage protection usually means a life insurance policy (most often term, sometimes whole life) sized to the mortgage balance and term, sometimes with living benefits or return of premium. The label is also used for other products, so the person should know which one it is:

- Credit life, often offered by the lender, pays the lender, not the family.
- Decreasing term has a death benefit that shrinks as the mortgage shrinks.
- Private mortgage insurance (PMI) protects the lender, not the family.

Always ask who the beneficiary is and whether the benefit shrinks. Some mailers for mortgage protection look official but are marketing from private companies, so comparing options with the agent is wise. Whatever the lead source, say clearly who you are, that you are an AI, and why you are in touch.

## Questions that help

- "How many years until the mortgage is paid off?"
- "How would the mortgage payments be covered if your income stopped?"
- "Does your coverage at work follow you if you change jobs?" (group coverage usually ends with the job and is often one or two times salary, though many plans allow a short conversion window)
- "Would you want the option to keep this coverage for life later?" (conversion)

## Lines that never move

No exact premium, no "you qualify", no comparison that disparages another carrier or another agent. The licensed agent quotes, compares and applies.
