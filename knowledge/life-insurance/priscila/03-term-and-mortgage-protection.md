# Term life and mortgage protection

Term life covers a person for a fixed number of years, commonly 10, 15, 20, 25 or 30. If they die during the term, the beneficiaries receive the death benefit. If the term ends, coverage ends unless it is renewed (usually at much higher yearly rates) or converted. It is the lowest cost per dollar of coverage, which makes it the usual answer for temporary, large needs: income replacement, a mortgage, children until they are independent, a business loan.

## Key features to explain

- Level premium: the premium stays the same for the whole term on a level term policy.
- Conversion: many term policies let the owner convert to a permanent policy without new medical questions, within a conversion window set by the carrier. Valuable if health changes later.
- Return of premium term: costs more, and refunds the premiums paid if the person outlives the term and kept the policy in force until the end. The refund conditions vary by carrier.
- Living benefits riders: many term policies can pay part of the death benefit early after a qualifying terminal, chronic or critical illness. See the living benefits document.
- Underwriting: from fully underwritten (exam, labs) to accelerated or no-exam options for healthier applicants within carrier limits. Rate classes go from preferred to standard and table ratings, with separate tobacco rates.

## How much coverage: a simple, transparent estimate

A common teaching method is DIME: Debts, plus Income (years the family would need it times annual income), plus Mortgage balance, plus Education costs, minus existing coverage and savings set aside. It is an educational estimate, never a recommendation. The licensed agent refines it with the person's real situation.

Example to explain the logic, not to quote: someone earning 50 thousand a year who wants to give the family 10 years of income, with a 200 thousand mortgage and 20 thousand of other debts, is looking at a need in the high hundreds of thousands before subtracting what they already have.

## Mortgage protection

Mortgage protection is not a separate kind of insurance. It is life insurance (usually term, sometimes whole life) sized to the mortgage balance and term, often with living benefits and sometimes return of premium. It is different from private mortgage insurance (PMI), which protects the lender, not the family. Mortgage protection leads frequently come from public mortgage records; be transparent about why you are calling or talking.

## Questions that help

- "How many years until the mortgage is paid off?"
- "If your income stopped, how long could the family keep the house?"
- "Does your coverage at work follow you if you change jobs?" (group coverage usually ends with the job and is often one or two times salary)
- "Would you want the option to keep this coverage for life later?" (conversion)

## Lines that never move

No exact premium, no "you qualify", no comparison that disparages another carrier or another agent. The licensed agent quotes, compares and applies.
