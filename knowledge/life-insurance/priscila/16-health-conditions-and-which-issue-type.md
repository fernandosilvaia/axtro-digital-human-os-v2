# Health conditions and which issue type fits

This is orientation, not a screening tool. Priscila never tells someone that they will or will not qualify. Different health histories point to different issue types, carriers draw the lines differently, and only the carrier decides after the application. The agent finds the carrier and product whose rules fit.

## How health shapes the path

- Fully underwritten policies look at the whole picture: application answers, an exam and labs for some amounts, prescription history, the MIB database and doctor records. Rate classes run from preferred to table ratings, with separate tobacco rates.
- Simplified issue asks a short list of health questions and checks prescription history instead of an exam. Some answers close the product, and some lead to a graded benefit with the first two years limited.
- Guaranteed issue asks no health questions. It costs more for the coverage and limits the benefit in the first two years.
- Prescription history, the MIB and motor vehicle records are common data sources, so honest answers matter: the carrier can see more than people expect.

## What the questions usually look at

- Recent events: a hospitalization, surgery or heart event in the last year or two, or a test or treatment that was recommended and not yet done.
- Cancer, with look-back windows that are often between two and four years or more.
- Conditions that affect daily living: needing help with bathing or dressing, using oxygen, a wheelchair or a nursing home.
- Chronic conditions such as diabetes (the age of diagnosis and complications often matter), lung disease and kidney disease.
- Tobacco and nicotine use. The agent confirms how the carrier counts cigars, vaping and nicotine products.

## Example from one carrier (illustration only)

One carrier's final expense product, according to a health question guide dated October 2024 and hosted by an agency, closes the product for heart disease or heart surgery in the past 12 months and for cancer in the past 2 years, and sends other answers to its graded benefit, such as cancer in the past 4 years or diabetes diagnosed before age 45. Other carriers use different questions and windows. Priscila never quotes a carrier's list as if it applied to the person.

## What Priscila says

- "Health history changes which options fit, and different companies treat the same condition differently. The agent will look at it with you and find the best match."
- If someone fears being turned down: "There are options for many health situations, including coverage with no health questions. The agent will explain how each one works and what it costs."
- Details of diagnoses and medications belong to the application with the licensed agent, not to this call.
