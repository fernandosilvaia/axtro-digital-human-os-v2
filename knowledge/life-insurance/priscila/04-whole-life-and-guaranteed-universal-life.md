# Whole life and guaranteed universal life (permanent coverage)

Permanent life insurance is designed to last for the person's whole life, not a set term. The two most common forms for families are whole life and guaranteed universal life (GUL). Both cost more than term for the same death benefit because the coverage is meant to pay out eventually.

## Whole life

- Level premiums for life and a death benefit that stays in force as long as premiums are paid.
- Cash value that grows on a schedule set in the contract. The guarantees depend on the insurer's claims-paying ability.
- Participating policies from mutual carriers may pay dividends. Dividends are never guaranteed; they can buy paid-up additions, reduce premiums or be taken in cash.
- Policy loans use the cash value as collateral. Loans accrue interest, and any unpaid loan reduces the death benefit. A large unpaid loan can make the policy lapse, which can create a tax bill.
- Surrendering early usually returns less than what was paid, because of costs in the first years.
- Best fit: lifelong needs (final expenses, legacy, a dependent with special needs, estate liquidity), people who value predictability and forced savings.

## Guaranteed universal life (GUL)

- Built to keep a death benefit in force to a chosen age, often 90, 95, 100 or beyond, at a lower premium than whole life.
- Little or no cash value; the point is the death benefit.
- The no-lapse guarantee depends on paying the planned premium on time. Paying late or less can shorten the guarantee.
- Best fit: someone who wants maximum lifetime coverage per premium dollar and does not care about cash value.

## How to explain the choice

- "Term is like renting coverage for the years you need it most. Permanent coverage is like owning it for life."
- Ask what matters more: the lowest premium today, coverage that never expires, or building cash value. The answer points to term, GUL or whole life.

## Lines that never move

- Never call cash value an investment or promise dividend amounts.
- Never say a permanent policy "pays for itself" without explaining the conditions.
