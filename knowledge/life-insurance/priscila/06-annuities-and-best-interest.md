# Annuities and the best interest review

An annuity is a contract with an insurance company, not life insurance. People use it to protect savings from market loss, to grow money tax deferred, or to turn a lump sum into income they cannot outlive. Contract guarantees depend on the insurer's claims-paying ability, and state guaranty associations provide limited protection that varies by state.

## The main types

- Multi-year guaranteed annuity (MYGA): a fixed interest rate for a set number of years, similar in spirit to a certificate of deposit but issued by an insurer. Withdrawals above the free amount during the term usually have surrender charges, and some contracts have a market value adjustment.
- Fixed indexed annuity (FIA): interest credits linked to an index, limited by caps, participation rates or spreads, with a floor so index losses are not credited. Surrender periods are often seven to ten years. Many allow around 10 percent penalty free withdrawals per year; the contract defines it.
- Income riders on FIAs (often called guaranteed lifetime withdrawal benefits): provide lifetime income based on an income base. The income base and its roll-up rate are used only to calculate income; they are not cash you can withdraw. Riders usually have a yearly fee.
- Single premium immediate annuity (SPIA): a lump sum turned into income that starts within a year, for life or for a period. Usually irrevocable.
- Deferred income annuity (DIA): income that starts years later, which raises the income amount.
- Qualified longevity annuity contract (QLAC): a deferred income annuity bought inside an IRA or qualified plan, within an IRS dollar limit that is adjusted over time, that can postpone required minimum distributions on that amount.
- Variable annuities: invested in subaccounts and regulated as securities. Discussing or recommending them requires a securities license: education only.

## Taxes and timing

Growth is generally tax deferred. Withdrawals of earnings are taxed as ordinary income, and withdrawals before age 59 and a half can carry an additional federal tax penalty. Money from 401(k) and IRA accounts keeps its qualified status if moved correctly, through a direct rollover or transfer. A 1035 exchange can move one annuity to another without current taxes, but surrender charges on the old contract can still apply.

## The best interest review (why Priscila never recommends an annuity)

Most states adopted the NAIC Suitability in Annuity Transactions Model Regulation with a best interest standard. Before recommending an annuity, the licensed agent must gather and document the consumer profile: age, annual income, financial situation and needs, financial experience, objectives, intended use of the annuity, time horizon, existing assets including other annuities and insurance, liquidity needs, liquid net worth, risk tolerance and tax status. The agent must have a reasonable basis that the recommendation addresses the person's needs and disclose relevant information. Many states add protections for seniors and a free look period after delivery.

## Questions that help (education only)

- "Is this money you might need in the next few years?"
- "What is it doing today: a CD, a savings account, an old 401(k)?"
- "Are you looking more for growth with protection, or for a paycheck in retirement?"
- "Would anyone else be part of this decision?"

## Lines that never move

- Never call an annuity a bank product or say it is FDIC insured.
- Never promise a rate, a return or an income amount; the licensed agent shows the contract figures.
- Never suggest moving money out of an existing annuity or retirement account; that decision requires the agent's review of surrender charges, taxes and suitability.
