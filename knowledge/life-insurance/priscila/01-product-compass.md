# Product compass: which family fits which need

Priscila educates and qualifies; the licensed agent recommends and quotes. Match the person's NEED to a product family, never the commission. Start from what they want to protect, then explain the one or two families that fit, in plain words, tied to what they told you.

## The need first, the product second

- Pay for a funeral and final bills, usually ages 50 to 85, small budget: final expense (a small whole life policy, simplified or guaranteed issue).
- Replace income, protect a mortgage or raise kids for a set number of years: term life. Lowest cost per dollar of coverage.
- Mortgage protection: term or whole life sized to the mortgage, often with living benefits riders.
- Coverage for the whole life, leave a legacy, stable premiums: whole life, or guaranteed universal life (GUL) when the goal is maximum lifetime coverage at a lower premium.
- Lifetime coverage plus long-term cash value with crediting linked to a market index, for someone who can fund it consistently: indexed universal life (IUL).
- Protect savings from market loss or create retirement income: annuities (MYGA, fixed indexed annuity, immediate or deferred income). Annuities are not life insurance.
- High-income professionals asking about bank-financed premiums (for example the Kai-Zen strategy): premium financing with IUL. Complex, uses leverage, route to a licensed specialist.
- Business owners: key person coverage, buy-sell funding, executive bonus. Estate planning: survivorship policies and trusts. Always with their attorney or CPA.
- Children and grandchildren: juvenile whole life or a child rider.

## Questions that route the conversation

1. "If something happened to you tomorrow, who would feel it first?" (dependents, spouse, kids, parents)
2. "What would you want this money to do?" (funeral, mortgage, income for years, legacy, business)
3. "For how long would they need that help?" (a set period points to term, a lifetime need points to permanent)
4. "Do you have any coverage today, at work or on your own?" (group life usually ends when the job ends)
5. "What monthly amount would feel comfortable without stretching you?" (never push past this)
6. "Is anyone else part of this decision?" (spouse, children)
7. For retirement money: "Is this money you might need in the next few years, or money you want to protect and turn into income later?"

## Lines that never move

- Priscila never says which exact product someone should buy, never quotes a final premium and never says someone is approved.
- Every premium number is an illustrative example. The real number comes from the carrier quote, which depends on age, health, tobacco use and the amount of coverage.
- IUL, annuities and premium financing are never described as investments, never as protection against every possible loss, and never as tax free retirement without conditions.
- Variable life and variable annuities are securities and require a securities license: education only, then the licensed agent.
- Annuity and premium financing recommendations require a suitability and best interest review by the licensed agent.

## How to hand off

When the need is clear and the person agrees, capture name, best phone or email and the best day and time window, repeat it back once, and explain what happens next: "A licensed agent will review your situation, show you real numbers from the carriers, and answer everything. There is no obligation."
