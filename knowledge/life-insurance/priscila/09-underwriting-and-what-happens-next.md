# Underwriting and what happens after this call

People relax when they know exactly what comes next. Priscila explains the path in simple steps and never says someone is approved, because only the carrier decides.

## The path

1. Conversation with the licensed agent: reviews needs and budget, shows real quotes from the carriers the agent works with. No obligation.
2. Application: completed with the licensed agent. This is where full health history, beneficiaries and payment details are collected, never on this call.
3. Underwriting: the carrier reviews the application. Depending on the product and amount, it may check prescription history, the MIB database of prior applications, driving records, and may ask for a phone interview, a paramedical exam with blood and urine samples, or records from the person's doctor.
4. Decision: the carrier approves as applied, approves with a different rate class, postpones or declines. Simplified issue products can decide in days; fully underwritten policies often take a few weeks.
5. Delivery and free look: after the policy is issued, there is a free look period to review and cancel for a refund. It is set by state law and the product: often 10 days, longer in some states and usually longer for replacements. Never give one number.

## Accelerated underwriting

Some carriers skip the exam and decide with outside data instead, such as prescription history, the MIB, motor vehicle records and credit-based information. That can cut a process of weeks to days or even hours. It does not always end in a policy: if the carrier has too little data, the person completes traditional underwriting, including the exam. Never promise an instant decision.

## Rate classes in plain words

Carriers group applicants by risk: preferred plus, preferred, standard plus, standard, and table ratings for higher risk, with separate tobacco rates. Height and weight, health conditions, family history, tobacco and nicotine use, and some hobbies or driving records can affect the class.

## Two clauses worth explaining

- Contestability period: usually the first two years. If the application had material misstatements, the carrier can review and contest a claim. That is why honest answers on the application protect the family.
- Suicide clause: usually the first two years (one year in a few states), the death benefit is limited to a refund of premiums.

## If a premium is missed

Policies have a grace period, commonly about a month, to pay after the due date. After that the policy can lapse. Many policies can be reinstated within a time limit, usually with back premiums and sometimes new health information, and a new contestability period may start. The agent explains the exact terms.

## What Priscila says

- "On the call with the agent you will see real numbers from the carriers the agent works with, and you decide with no pressure."
- "The application asks about your health in detail, so the company has accurate information if a claim is ever made."
- If asked "Am I approved?": "Only the insurance company decides that, after the application. My job is to make sure the agent already has your full picture."
